## Resolved cross-cutting finding from the adversarial pass (2026-07-22)
The `ctx.reflected_control_max_count == 0` branch at `receiver/mod.rs:1807` does short-circuit before the `req.number_of_reflected_packets == 0` branch at line 1816 — that part of the original finding was correct, and it does produce a real, wire-visible difference (one reply packet sent vs. zero) relative to the draft's literal §3 default. What was missing from the original write-up was §3's very next sentence: "a local policy MAY override this default behavior and specify an alternative handling" (asym-3-17). `--reflected-control-max-count` defaulting to 0 (mandated off-by-default by asym-5-02) *is* that local policy, and its alternative handling — always answer with a single, non-amplified, C=1-flagged reply, regardless of the requested count, including 0 — is exactly what the disabled branch implements, and was deliberately hardened to do so uniformly (see `tests/tlv_flag_semantics.rs::reflected_control_length_padding_disabled_by_default`, "Security (audit finding 1)"). So the `if/else if` precedence is not a defect: it is the concrete realization of asym-3-17's escape valve winning over asym-3-15/16's default, which the MAY clause explicitly permits. asym-3-15, asym-3-16, and asym-3-17 have all been updated to Compliant accordingly. The only remaining, non-blocking suggestion is a regression test that exercises `count=0` together with `max_count=0` explicitly (today only `count=8` is exercised in the disabled-by-default tests), to lock in that the two independently-tested branches compose as expected.
